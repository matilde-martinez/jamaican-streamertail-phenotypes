# Precisely quantifying Jamaican streamertail phenotypes in the world's narrowest avian hybrid zone

Red-billed and black-billed streamertails (Trochilus polytmus, Trochilus scitulus) are sister species of hummingbirds endemic to Jamaica. These two species are known to hybridize, producing offspring that have intermediate bills displaying an array of colors intermediate between the red and black coloration typical of the parental species. Previous studies propose that bill color acts as a reproductive barrier, possibly because red-billed adults will not mate with black-billed or hybrid birds, whose bills resemble those of young red-billed birds that have not yet reached sexual maturity. This (or other) putative barriers must provide incredibly strong reproductive isolating mechanisms, as this hybrid zone has been characterized as the narrowest avian hybrid zone in the world. To investigate these reproductive isolating mechanisms, we used a transect of 204 museum specimens from across Jamaica to precisely characterize the phenotypic transition in bill color. Previous work in this hybrid zone used a categorical scoring system to assign each specimen a score based on the color of their bill (1 for black-billed, 2 and 3 for hybrids, 4 and 5 for red-billed). We re-scored these 204 museum specimens using this approach and noticed differences between observers, highlighting the subjectivity of this categorical scoring system. We improved upon this approach by quantifying the proportion of red versus black in each bird’s bill in a continuous manner. Using photos in ImageJ, we measured the length of the dorsal and ventral bills for each specimen, as well as the length of any red coloration. We calculated the proportion of red in each bird’s bill by dividing the length of the red sections by the length of the entire bill. Additionally, we used the R package recolorize as an independent, automated scoring approach to extract the RGB values of every pixel in the bill. From this, we divided the number of identified red pixels by the total number of pixels in the bill to compute the percentage of red. This proved especially useful for analyzing hybrid bills with non-continuous, red splotches that were difficult to measure in ImageJ. Now, we are connecting these more accurate, continuous measures of bill phenotype with genomic data to understand the basis of reproductive isolating barriers in this fascinating system.
